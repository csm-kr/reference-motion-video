# Genres: beat templates, targets, references

From a research pass on commercial product and concept films (2026-10-18). Tags: **[S]** cited source, **[M]** measured from the official YouTube captions (words ÷ runtime), **[E]** estimate; measure the actual footage before treating an [E] as a target.

## Five genres

| genre | beats in order | 30 s | 60 s | 2-3 min | palette |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Feature launch** | product or UI already moving (no intro card) → name + promise → features as claim card + proof shot → montage on the drop → logo / CTA | 0-3 / 3-6 / 3×6 s / 24-30 | 0-4 / 4-8 / 6×7 s / 50-60 | 25-30 s chapters, recap, 10 s end | `drums_launch` |
| **B. Use-case vignette** | a person mid-moment → friction → asks the AI (the prompt is the hero line) → the answer acts → human payoff → line + logo | 0-5 / 5-12 / 12-22 / 22-26 / 26-30 | 2-3 escalating asks, logo 54-60 | three vignettes, or one story with 4-6 asks | needs live action |
| **C. Concept explainer** | analogy or question hook → the problem in one picture → mechanism in 3 steps ("so... but... so") → proof on real output → why it matters → CTA | 0-4 / 4-20 / 20-26 / 26-30 | 0-5 / 5-12 / 12-40 / 40-52 / 52-60 | 0-10 / 10-30 / 30-100 / 100-140 / 140-165 | `soft_pulse` |
| **D. Demo walkthrough** | the outcome first (2-3 s) → setup → task 1 in real time → a harder task 2 (follow-up, interruption, memory) → a surprise task 3 → recap + availability | one task | two tasks | 3-5 labelled tasks | `bright_demo` |
| **E. Manifesto / documentary** | an image hook, no product → the thesis line → people making or living → the product late, in their hands → the thesis returns → logo | n/a | product in the second half, logo in the last 5-8 s | person 0-15, stakes 15-45, product 45-120, payoff, logo | needs live action |

Anchors with sources: TV-ad shots averaged under 2 s already in 1991 (MacLachlan & Logan, WARC). Google's ABCD guide (5,000+ ads): more than two frames and the brand in the first 5 s, tight framing, people first, the logo inside the story, the call to action audible and visible (services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf). A pro read runs about 2.5 words a second, 70-80 words per 30 s. Adults read up to 20 characters a second (Netflix timed-text guide).

## What separates them from amateur demos

- **Copy:** one idea per card, short present-tense lines to "you". Hooks name a tension, not a feature ("Any phone looks impressive in a shiny commercial. But... under your bathroom sink?"). The voice-over explains by cause and effect ("More power creates more heat. So...").
- **Type and motion:** no intro card; the camera does the transitions (zoom in, whip, hold on the payoff); screens bleed off the frame; easing from motion tokens; reading holds long enough for 20 characters a second.
- **Sound:** music chosen like a film score; in demos the real voice and room dominate; one sonic sign-off at the end.
- **People:** faces first; keep imperfection ("let me practice that one again"); a reaction after each AI answer.
- **Showing an AI conversation:** (1) the prompt as hero type, the answer as rolling credits, no UI; (2) a real prompt box, a typed prompt, the result changing in place; (3) for voice, the assistant heard while the screen shows a quiet listening sign and large captions; (4) AI highlights drawn over the live camera view; (5) the assistant as a character with comic timing; (6) honest time compression, labelled.

## Without filming

Genres A, C and D can be made in full with HTML/GSAP and real captures (kinetic type, a virtual camera over captures, diagrams, prompt type, listening signs, captions, generic device frames, a composed score, TTS). B and E need faces, hands and rooms. The cheapest legal routes: film your own hands and rooms with a signed release from anyone who appears; Pexels clips (commercial use, no credit, no implied endorsement, check frames for logos); stills made in ChatGPT and animated with parallax (OpenAI assigns the output to you). Paid video generation (Veo from $0.05-0.40 a second) needs a YouTube label when realistic; Sora's app and API are closed.
